LLM Judge Identity Bias

Does telling a judge model who wrote a response change how it scores that response, independent of actual quality?

Experiment Design GitHub
TL;DR
Revealing identity depresses scores — regardless of which name you use
Across 1,260 judgments, all three attribution conditions (true, upward mis-attribution, downward mis-attribution) scored significantly lower than blind evaluation. The direction of mis-attribution barely mattered. Blind evaluation was consistently the most generous. This suggests LLM-as-judge pipelines that include model names in the prompt introduce a systematic downward bias.

Key Findings
1 — Any identity disclosure hurts, direction doesn't matter
Upward mis-attribution (−0.136) was slightly worse than downward (−0.082) — the opposite of what a prestige-boost hypothesis would predict. The asymmetry is not statistically significant. The consistent pattern is: naming a model depresses the score, regardless of whether that name is higher or lower tier than the true author.
2 — Factual tasks are most affected
Downward delta by task: factual −0.141 (CI does not cross zero), analytical −0.077, creative −0.029. Creative tasks are nearly immune to identity disclosure. Factual tasks may be more susceptible because judges have clearer expectations about what a given model "should" know.
3 — GPT-4.1 Mini shows near-zero bias as a judge
GPT-4.1 Mini's downward delta was −0.017 — an order of magnitude smaller than GPT-4o Mini (−0.111) and GPT-4.1 (−0.117). This was confirmed across 5 reps. The mechanism is unknown — it may reflect more robust instruction-following or reduced sensitivity to contextual priming.
4 — Attribution rarely reverses rankings
Only 5.9% of pairs where blind scoring favoured the higher-tier model were flipped by attribution. Identity disclosure shifts scores systematically but infrequently overturns relative ordering.
Caveats & Open Questions
GPT-5 Mini judge data is unreliable — the API does not support temperature control for this model, forcing temperature=1 for all judgments. Sample size is also small (n=9 for downward).

Edge tier coverage asymmetry — GPT-4o Mini (tier 1) has no downward condition; GPT-5 Mini (tier 4) has no upward. Upward/downward headline comparisons pool slightly different model subsets. The Wilcoxon asymmetry test controls for this by using matched pairs.

Mechanism unknown — identity disclosure may cause anchoring (name sets an expectation the response fails to meet), distraction (extra context degrades judgment quality), or expectation mismatch. A fictional model name condition would disambiguate.

Why is GPT-4.1 Mini robust? The zero-bias finding is the most surprising result and warrants follow-up.
